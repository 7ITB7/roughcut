Rough Cut

Finds what's about to be worth more, before the market notices

📈 Fame Radar · Before Fame

People whose Wikipedia traffic just exploded (a breakout, a viral moment, a call-up, a casting) but whose early stuff hasn't been repriced yet: yearbooks from their school, first records, college programs, rookie cards, early signed items.

The play: open "Cheapest now" and compare with "Sold". If listings are priced below recent sales, or nobody has listed the early items yet, buy before collectors catch on.

🎬 Page-to-Screen

Books with a film or TV adaptation coming out, or just released. First editions of the source book often jump when the trailer or show lands, and by then it's too late.

The play: buy true first printings, especially signed or with a dust jacket, while the release is still months away. Sell into the hype. Check "AbeBooks 1st eds" for supply.

⚾ Prospect Pulse

Young minor leaguers (23 and under) putting up big numbers at AA and AAA who haven't debuted in MLB yet. Their 1st Bowman cards are cheapest before the call-up.

The play: target their "1st Bowman" base, chrome or refractor cards. Buy in the offseason or early spring; the call-up is usually the sell moment.

💸 Refund Gap · Recall Arbitrage

New U.S. product recalls (CPSC) where the maker refunds owners. When used units sell for less than the refund, the gap is the profit.

Rules: read the remedy first, because some refunds need a receipt or are limited to original buyers. Never resell a recalled item; claim the refund and dispose of it as instructed. Only count it as a lead if the refund terms allow it.

🎯 Resolution Watch

Polymarket markets where the answer may already be known: the deadline has passed or the outcome has been proposed, but the price hasn't hit 100% yet. Plus markets in their final 48 hours with a strong favorite.

The play: open the resolution source and check it yourself. If the answer is already official, buying the winning side at 90–97¢ pays the gap when it settles. Not risk-free: rules can have fine print, results can be disputed, and settlement can take days. Read the rules every time and size small. (Kalshi blocks outside apps, so check Kalshi by hand.)

💿 First Pressing Finder

Look up any album. See every pressing in order, the first pressing's matrix/runout codes, and the price gap between originals and reissues. Then use the codes to spot first pressings that sellers listed as cheap reissues.

The play: at thrift stores and on listings, read the letters etched in the run-out groove (the smooth part near the label) and paste them in "Check a record". If they match the first pressing, you've got the valuable one.

🎤 Royalty Hunter

Finds musicians from your state who've released music but show no songwriter ID (IPI) or ISNI in MusicBrainz. That often means they never registered with a PRO, SoundExchange or The MLC, so royalties may be piling up unclaimed.

The play: check them on the SoundExchange and MLC searches. If money is waiting, offer a flat-fee "I'll get you registered and paid" service. Be upfront that registering is free and they can do it themselves; you're selling the legwork.

🌐 Expired Domains · Backlink Graveyard

Wikipedia articles cite thousands of websites. When those sites die, their domains often go unregistered, but the Wikipedia links (and others around the web) still point at them. That's built-in authority and traffic that SEO buyers pay for.

The play: register "AVAILABLE" domains for about $10 and resell them on Sedo, Afternic or SEO marketplaces, or build a site on them. Never pose as the old organization or reuse its brand.

🔬 Lab Salvage

Universities, hospitals and government labs dump working equipment for pennies. The same gear resells to labs, schools and hobbyists for hundreds to thousands. These sites can't be scanned automatically, so these are one-tap searches.

The play: check the surplus searches weekly. Before bidding, tap the model's "sold" link to confirm resale value. Brand plus exact model number is everything.

🏦 Escheat Auctions

When safe-deposit boxes go unclaimed, states take the contents and periodically auction off the coins, stamps, jewelry, watches and stock certificates, often in vague lots that few bidders understand.

The play: find your target state's next auction, browse the lots for coins, stamps and certificates, and price them before bidding. Old certificates? Run them through Paper Value.
